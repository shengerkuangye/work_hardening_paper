Abstract
The deformation behavior of commercially pure titanium is studied using experiments and a
crystal plasticity model. Compression tests along the rolling, transverse, and normaldirections, and tensile tests along the rolling and transverse directions are performed at room
temperature to study the activation of slip and twinning in the hexagonal closed packed
titanium. A detailed EBSD based statistical analysis of the microstructure is performed to
develop statistics of both {10-12} tensile and {11-22} compression twins. A simple Monte
Carlo (MC) twin variant selection criterion is proposed within the framework of the viscoplastic self-consistent (VPSC) model with a dislocation density (DD) based law used to
describe dislocation hardening. In the model, plasticity is accommodated by prismatic, basal
and pyramidal slip modes, and {10-12} tensile and {11-22} compression twinning modes.
The VPSC-MC model successfully captures the experimentally observed activation of low
Schmid factor twin variants for both tensile and compression twins modes. The model also
predicts macroscopic stress-strain response, texture evolution and twin volume fraction that
are in agreement with experimental observations.
Keywords: crystal plasticity, twin variant selection, titanium, dislocation density
1. Introduction
Titanium and its alloys are used in several industries, including automotive and
aerospace, due to their high specific strength. A lack of available crystallographic slip
systems in hexagonal close packed (HCP) titanium necessitates the activation of twinning to
accommodate any arbitrary deformation [1-3]. In HCP titanium, {10-12} twins normally
accommodate extension along the c-axis and are frequently referred to as tensile twins, and

1

{11-22} twins normally accommodate contraction along the c-axis and are frequently
referred to as compression twins. While dislocation slip is rather homogeneous over the
crystals and involves gradual lattice reorientation, deformation twins are localized, lamellar
shaped domains within the crystal resulting in a significant lattice reorientation [3-5]. For
instance, in titanium with the c/a ratio of 1.588, the lattice rotation and local shear strain
associated with {10-12} <0111> tensile twins is approximately 85o and 17.4%, respectively.
The heterogeneous twinning microstructure greatly affects the polycrystal deformation
behavior through: i) reorientation to a harder or softer orientation resulting in the activation
of different deformation modes, ii) a Hall-Petch effect due to twin induced grain subdivision,

considered explicitly to study the deformation behavior of HCP titanium.
Deformation twinning is a sequential process of creation of an embryo (nucleation),
propagation of this embryo as a lamella into the crystal or grain, and subsequent thickening
(growth) of the twin lamella. Each of these events is stochastic and depends on several
factors including microstructural features, internal stresses, direction and rate of loading,
strain level, and temperature [4,11-14]. For example, the work of Bieler et al. [4] in pure
titanium using in-situ x-ray diffraction shows that the highest resolved shear stress (RSS)
twin variant is activated in a grain surrounded by hard grain orientations; whereas low RSS
twin variants are activated in grains surrounded by ‘soft’ neighbor orientations.
Understanding of twin variant selection is important because it affects the microstructural
evolution, such as the formation of parallel twins and/or twin-twin intersections, and then it
controls the overall deformation behavior and texture evolution [15]. Most models rely on
the average stress state in the grain and a maximum Schmid factor criterion for twin variant
selection. However, twin variant activation depends on local stress fluctuations inside the
grain and near grain boundaries, and a systematic study is required to establish the statistical
distribution of twin variants in a particular material system. Extensive statistical analysis
providing the distribution of twin variants in HCP Mg and Zr have been reported [11-12].
An equivalent study for titanium, however, is not available. In this work we report the
statistical distribution of twin variants in HCP commercially pure titanium obtained by
employing the recently developed automated twinning analysis software METIS to analyze
EBSD images of the deformed microstructure [16-17].
Understanding the deformation mechanisms including twinning activity and twin
variant selection, requires a predictive crystal plasticity model. Over several decades, several
2

experimental and computational modeling efforts have been dedicated to understand
deformation mechanisms and their effect on the material response of titanium and its alloys
[8-9, 18-29]. Most of the experimental literature on titanium concerns the effect of twinning
on mechanical anisotropy and strain hardening [5, 8-9, 24, 30-31], and on failure
mechanisms [32-33]. A few studies also focus on the effect of interstitial alloying elements
on deformation twinning [34].
In what concerns crystal plasticity modeling of Ti, some relevant studies have been
performed to understand the deformation mechanisms. The crystal plasticity finite element
(CP-FE) approach has been extensively used to study the deformation mechanisms and
associated behavior of HCP titanium [9, 24, 26, 28, 31]. The CP-FE studies connect the
observed strain hardening behavior and texture evolution to twin and slip activities, and
most use an empirical hardening equation like the Voce law. Recent work of Zecevic et al.
[29] analyzes the deformation mechanism using CP-FE coupled with a dislocation density
based hardening law. The few studies based on the effective medium visco-plastic selfconsistent (VPSC) model rely on an empirical hardening law [24, 26, 35]. All of these CPFE and VPSC works treat twining in Ti as a deterministic process, and neglect the
experimentally observed statistical variations. A stochastic twinning model has been
previously developed for HCP Mg and Zr [36-38] that probabilistically accounts for
variation in the microstructural and mechanical responses in order to capture the
experimentally observed statistical variations in twin nucleation and growth. Successful
application of such a stochastic twinning model for titanium requires knowledge of the
distribution of microstructural features such as grain size, grain boundary facet area, number
of grain boundaries; and the local stress fluctuations due to inter-grain interactions and preexisting twins [38-42]. All of these distributions need to be incorporated in probabilistic
manner leadings to several model parameters. To avoid this cumbersome undertaking, in this
work we propose a simple Monte Carlo based twin variant selection model to capture the
experimentally observed twinning statistics.
In this work, we perform a systematic experimental and computational modeling to
understand the deformation mechanisms in rolled commercially pure titanium with a splitbasal initial texture. Compression and tension tests are performed along rolling, normal and
transverse directions to activate tensile and compressive twinning modes. An automated
EBSD twinning analysis software (METIS) is used to analyze and establish the statistical
twin variant distributions [16-17]. On the modeling side we develop a simple Monte Carlo
twin variant selection scheme, and implement it within the framework of VPSC. Model
3

parameters are calibrated by fitting the experimentally measured stress-strain response,
texture evolution and twin volume fraction evolution. The agreement between observed and
predicted twin variant distribution validates the proposed Monte Carlo twin variant selection
scheme.

Crystal plasticity modeling
To study the underlying active deformation mechanisms of CP-Ti and to provide an

insight into the observed twinning and strain hardening behaviors, here we employed a viscoplastic self-consistent (VPSC) model. A comprehensive description of the VPSC model
originally developed by Lebensohn and Tome [43] can be found in Lebensohn et al [44]. In
Sections 3.1 and 3.2, the polycrystal model and dislocation density based hardening law for
slip and twinning systems will be briefly described. The Monte Carlo scheme used for twin
variant selection and reorientation is novel and will be described in Section 3.2.

3.3 Variant selection and twin reorientation model
An aspect of twinning that needs to be incorporated into the models is the fact that
twins are domains inside the grain (usually of lamellar morphology) with a different
orientation than the surrounding matrix. These twinned regions contribute to the texture of
the aggregate and also act as effective barriers for the propagation of dislocations or other
twin lamellae. Based on the practical limitation that simulations cannot treat every twin as a
new orientation, in the past we have implemented a Predominant Twin Reorientation (PTR)
scheme [49] in the VPSC framework to account for twinning reorientation. The PTR criterion
consists on finding the most active twin system in the grain, and reorienting the whole grain
according to such system, provided other conditions are also met. In this work we introduce a
variation of the PTR model based on a Monte-Carlo (MC) selection criterion of the active
twin variant which, while favoring the predominant twin system, may also predict
reorientation on less favorable twin variants.

The result can be interpreted as an empirical way to capture the role-played by stress
fluctuations inside grains in activating twinning. The MC scheme is an efficient, although
empirical, way to introduce fluctuations not addressed by the effective medium approach in
which VPSC is based. Niezgoda et al. [38] and Arul Kumar et al. [36] have proposed
methods for introducing stress fluctuations at grain boundaries and at grain interiors,
respectively, based on computationally complex preliminary calculations of distributions.

6. Discussion
Implications of MC scheme: Several studies have been dedicated to understanding the twin
variant selection process in HCP Ti [28, 51-54] using different criterions such as
minimization of deformation energy, neighboring grain slip and/or twin induced twin
nucleation, and neighboring grain accommodation of twinning shear. The most common
conclusion in all of these studies is that local stress fluctuations and the microstructural
heterogeneity in the polycrystal cause the activation of low Schmid factor twin variants for
both tensile and compression twin modes. Several local processes can develop stress
fluctuations in a grain such as i) grain-grain interactions; ii) neighboring grain twins induced
local stresses; iii) non-uniform stresses induced by dislocation interactions. The VPSC model
cannot realize such local intra-granular mechanisms, and so they have to be incorporated into
the model in an ad-hoc fashion. As mentioned in Sec. 1, a complex twin stochastic model was
developed to explicitly account for the local stress fluctuations and microstructural
heterogeneity [36-38]. But in this work, we have introduced a stochasticity associated with
twinning via a simple Monte Carlo scheme for twin variant selection. The arbitrary random
selection used to identify the twin variant indirectly represents the twinning probability
associated with local stress fluctuations and microstructural heterogeneities. Even if the
proposed MC scheme simplifies the complex twinning process into a simple random number
selection, it succeeds qualitatively in activating the statistically correct twin variants for both
tensile and compression twin modes as seen in Fig. 5.
Low SF variants are more likely for compression twin mode than tensile twin mode: The
detailed EBSD based statistical analysis and the model predictions of twin variant distribution
shown in Fig. 5 suggest that more low variant twins are activated for {11-22} compression
twins compared to {10-12} tensile twins. It can be shown, via a simple Schmid factor
analysis, that the reason is the relative inclination of twin variants with respect to loading. For

Summary
The deformation behavior of HCP CP-titanium was studied using experimental and
computational modeling, with an emphasis on understanding twin variant activation and
statistics. Tensile and compression tests along different directions relative to a split basal
texture were performed to study the stress-strain response and microstructure and texture
evolution. EBSD based statistical of twinning in the deformed microstructures was performed
to establish the distribution of twin variants. The VPSC model was used to simulate the
deformation behavior, and a simple Monte Carlo twin variant selection scheme was proposed
to account for the stochasticity associated with twinning. The proposed VPSC-MC model
captures the experimentally measured stress-strain response, texture evolution, twin volume
fraction evolution and twin variants statistics.
