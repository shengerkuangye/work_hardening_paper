and BP Neural Network Models to Predict
Hot Deformation Behaviors of a
Hypereutectoid Steel

 1, YONG DENG 2, YUAN WANG 3, AND JINGCHUAN ZHU 1
1School of Material Science and Engineering, Harbin Institute of Technology, Harbin 150001, China

ABSTRACT In order to predict the high-temperature deformation behavior of hypereutectoid steel, the hot
compression tests were conducted in the strain rate range of (0.001∼1) s−1 and the deformation temperature
range of (950∼1100)◦C. The experimental data were employed to develop the Arrhenius constitutive model
and BP neural network model, and their predictability for high temperature ﬂow stress of hypereutectoid steel
was further evaluated. Comparatively, a higher correlation coefﬁcient ( R) can be obtained for the BP neural
network model compared with the Arrhenius constitutive equations. And the relative error within ±1% was
more than 68.75% for the BP neural network model, while only 18.75% for the constitutive equations. The
BP neural network model is considered more efﬁcient and accurate to predict the hot deformation behavior
than the Arrhenius constitutive equations. Moreover, the well-trained BP neural network model is employed
to predict the ﬂow stress varying with the deformation temperature and strain rate. The ﬂow stress decreases
with the increasing deformation temperature and decreasing strain rate, which is in accordance with the
experimental evaluation. The results indicate that BP neural network model is an efﬁcient tool for modelling
and predicting the ﬂow behavior of hypereutectoid steels in high temperature applications.

I. INTRODUCTION
Hypereutectoid steel is widely applied in many industrial
ﬁelds for its great mechanical property, especially in the ﬁeld
of rail technology [1]–[3]. The understanding of the ﬂow
behaviors of steels has become more essential to researchers
engaged in hot deformation condition. To improve the
mechanical performance of hypereutectoid steels, an explo-
ration of the deformation behavior at high temperature has
become a critical issue.
Knowledge of the hot deformation mechanisms is consid-
ered to be an essential prerequisite for the optimization of
material properties [4]. However, the researches on the hyper-
eutectoid steel is rare and this particular subject needs to be

higher accuracy of ANN model. Since the artiﬁcial neural
network learns from the experimental data and identify the
relevant parameters and their interactions in a series of input
and output values without material constants, it has become
a powerful tool to establish the constitutive relationship and
further predict the ﬂow behaviors during hot compression
process.
In present paper, hot compression tests were performed
to obtain the rheological curves. Subsequently, constitutive
relationships based on the Arrhenius constitutive equation
and a three-layer feed-forward ANN model are employed
to characterize the hot deformation behavior of the hyper-
eutectoid steels. And the suitability of these two models to
predict the hot deformation behavior was evaluated. Finally,
the coupled effects of the deformation temperature and strain
rate on the ﬂow behavior were systematically investigated
and provided a reference for the actual industrial application.
II. EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURE
The material used in this study was hypereutectoid steel
and the chemical composition (wt%) was plotted in Table.1.
Then these materials were processed into cylindrical speci-
mens with a diameter of 6 mm and a height of 9 mm. The
hot compression tests were carried out on a Gleeble-1500D
thermo-mechanical simulator in the strain rate of 1 s −1,
0.1 s−1, 0.01 s−1, 0.001s−1 and the deformation temperature
of 950◦C, 1000◦C, 1050◦C, 1100◦C, respectively. To mini-
mize the deformed friction between the specimens and simu-
lator, the graphite was used as lubricant mixed with machine
oil. Each sample was heated to the deformation temperature
at a rate of 10 K/s and the samples are homogenized for 3 min
before compression. During the examination, a thermocouple
at the center of specimen was used to monitor the temper-
ature while the stress–strain data at different temperatures

the deformation in height was 75% at the end of compression

III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSIONS
A. FLOW BEHAVIOR ANAL YSIS
Fig.1 depicts the typical true stress–strain curves obtained
from the hot compression tests of the hypereutectoid steel.
Apparently, the evolution of all the ﬂow stress curves follows
the same trend. With the work hardening at the initial stage
of deformation, the ﬂow stresses are increasing rapidly with
the increase of strain and reach a peak value [28]. Attributed
to the dynamic recrystallization, the softening effect con-
tributes to a slight decrease in the ﬂow stress. Until arriv-
ing at a balance of the work hardening and ﬂow softening,
subsequently, a steady state was drawn for the ﬂow stress
at the higher strain. Accordingly, the ﬂow stress tends to be
intensely dependent on the forming temperature and strain
rate under the working conditions. It is found that the ﬂow
stress decreases with the increase in deformation tempera-
tures for a given strain rate. The increasing temperature would
decrease the critical shear stress of each slip system and
increase the thermal activation energy, thus contributing to the
dynamic recovery recrystallization. While for a speciﬁc tem-
perature, the ﬂow stress generally increases with the increase
in strain rates due to an increase of dislocation density and
the dislocation multiplication rate. Moreover, the increasing
strain rate tends to result in the inhomogeneity of plastic
deformation and there is no enough time for the necessary
deformation [29].

IV. CONCLUSION
The present study analyzed the hot deformation behavior
of hypereutectoid steel, investigated by a series of thermal
deformation tests in the strain rate range of (0.001∼1) s−1 and
the deformation temperature range of (950 ∼1100) C◦. The
experimental stress–strain data were employed to develop
the Arrhenius constitutive equations and the BP neural net-
work model. And a comparative investigation was carried
out on their predictability of the hot deformation behav-
ior of hypereutectoid steel. The conclusions can be drawn
as follows.
(1) The predictability of the Arrhenius constitutive equa-
tions and BP neural network model was further evaluated
in terms of the correlation coefﬁcient (R) and the relative
error(η). For the Arrhenius constitutive equations, R value
is determined as 0.993, while for the BP neural network
model it is found to be 0.995, respectively. The relative errors
between experimental and predicted ﬂow stress obtained
from the constitutive equations and BP neural network model
are in the range of −8.6%∼13.9% and −10.5% to 7.9%,
respectively. Moreover, the relative error within ±1% was
observed for more than 68.75% for the BP neural net-
work model, while only 18.75% for the constitutive equa-
tions. Thus, the developed BP neural network model has
better capability compared with the Arrhenius constitutive
equations.
(2) The BP neural network model for hypereutectoid steel
can accurately predict the ﬂow stress behavior across a wider
range of temperatures and strain rates. The coupled effects
of the deformation temperature and strain rate on the ﬂow
behavior were systematically investigated and the evolution
indicates that the ﬂow stress decreases with the increasing
deformation temperature and decreasing strain rate. Thus, the
developed BP neural network model could not only quite well
predict the hot deformation behavior but also signiﬁcantly
reduce time and cost for the analyses.

JINGCHUAN ZHU received the B.S. degree in
metalology and heat treatment from Chongqing
University, Chongqing, China, in 1982, and the
M.S. and Ph.D. degrees in metal materials and heat
treatment from the Harbin Institute of Technology,
Harbin, Heilongjiang, China, in 1991 and 1994,
respectively.
He is currently a Full Professor with the School
of Materials Science and Engineering, Harbin
Institute of Technology. He is the author who has
more than 100 articles and two books. He has also more than ten patents
in the area of materials. His research interest includes multi scale computer
simulation and design of materials, functionally gradient composite material
design, and so on. He is a Senior Member of China Metal Society, the Deputy
Secretary General of Heilongjiang Materials and Testing Society, and the
Executive Vice President of Heilongjiang Metal Society.
